Stand-in for Asimov 1's hung upper body (all mm; x forward, y left, z up; origin = the centre of mass, which hangs on the hook's axis)

Cost: about 60 to 90 USD of plywood, bar, plate and hook, plus about 1 h of print time for the cover (117 g PA12 or PETG).

TORSO BOX, 12 mm birch plywood, glued and screwed, outer 220 (x) x 250 (y) x 380 (z), back open.
  Front panel 12 x 250 x 380 at x +105.5 to +117.5.  Sides 2 x (220 x 12 x 380).  Top and bottom 2 x (220 x 226 x 12).
  Box spans x -102.5 to +117.5, y +-125, z -169 to +212 from the centre of mass (the torso hulls in Menlo's URDF: x -102 to +117, y +-125, z -169 to +212).
COVER SEAT (the back-cover interface). The cover, Menlo part 100_04C, is printed from ASV1_100_04C.STEP
  (github.com/menloresearch/asimov-1, mechanical/ASV1/100/FABRICATION/MJF_PA12/, CERN-OHL-S-2.0): 98 x 224 x 315 mm, 117 g, 8 M4 bolt holes,
  handle bar and slot. Export the STEP to STL (FreeCAD or any CAD), keep the robot's orientation, print in PA12 or PETG, 3 perimeters, 20 % infill.
  It sits inside the box's open back: the box's inner width 226 and height 356 take its 224 x 315 outline. Its seated place is the cover.npz pose:
  its front rim at x +7, its back face at x -91.  Glue 12 x 10 mm plywood stops on the inside of the 2 sides, the top and the bottom with their
  faces at x +7 (a 340 mm strip on each side, a 200 mm strip top and bottom); the cover is pressed in until its rim meets them, as the scene's bay stops it.
  Bolts are left out, as in the scene. For a fit with bolts, drill the 8 holes through the printed cover (3.3 mm pilot into the stops) in 
  place and fit M4 heat-set inserts; the task needs none.
BALLAST (the "battery"): 7 mild-steel plates 10 x 100 x 100 (0.785 kg each, 5.495 kg), stacked along x from x +25 to +95 and centred at z +70,
  y 0. Rest on 2 plywood cleats 20 x 12 x 70 screwed to the sides (top face at z +20), and strap with a cable tie or an M8 threaded rod through the plates and the sides.
HEAD: pine block 90 (x) x 130 (y) x 180 (z), centre x +12, z +302 (it sits on the top panel), screwed to the top panel (2 x No 10 wood screws up through the top). Mass 1.05 kg.
ARMS: 2 bars of 60 (x) x 20 (y) x 450 (z) mild-steel flat, 4.24 kg each; centre x -53, y +-137, z -90 (top at z +135, hands' end at z -315). 
  Bolt each to the side with 2 M8 x 40 bolts at z +40 and z -50 through the side panel (washers as spacers so the bar's face clears the panel by 2 mm).
  They are rigid, as the scene locks the arms straight.
HANG: 2 forged M8 eyebolts through the top panel at x +8, y +-90, z +212; 2 chain legs of 250 mm (6 mm chain, 1 t WLL) to a ring on the hook.
  Hook: Yoke G80 swivel eye sling hook with ball bearing, 7/8 mm chain size, supplier code 113308 as listed at aimsindustrial.com.au; check the working load limit in the supplier's table is at least 1 t; any ball-bearing
  swivel sling hook of 1 t or more works, e.g. Crosby L-3322B 5 t, Gunnebo LKNK, Certex X100). Hang it from a beam hook or shackle on the gantry beam,
  so that the centre of mass (budget.py) is 350 mm above the table at (450, 0).
TRIM (do this before the first trial): weigh the finished stand-in on a luggage or crane scale (+-50 g): it must read 18.3 +-0.2 kg (add or remove fender washers
  on the head; the eyebolts, screws and chain are weighed in with it, so the washers make up the rest). Hang it: it should hang level, the top panel horizontal within 1 degree, with its front facing the arms; if it tilts, slide the ballast
  stack along x. Then run the free spin-down (start about 40 deg/s, time it) as the README says.
Yaw inertia check, optional: hang it on a 3-wire (bifilar) or one thin-wire torsion pendulum and time 10 swings; I = T^2 m g r^2 / (4 pi^2 L) for the bifilar of wire spacing 2r and length L.
Budget and checks: python standin/budget.py (mass 18.09 + 0.19 kg of washers = 18.28 kg, centre of mass within 0.5 mm, yaw inertia 0.259 vs 0.257 kg m2, +1.0 %).
