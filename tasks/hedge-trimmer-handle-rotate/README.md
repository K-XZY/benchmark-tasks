# Turn a hedge trimmer's rear handle to its 90 degree detent

Two arms set a cordless hedge trimmer up for side cutting, as its maker's manual prescribes: press the release button, draw the 1.24 kg battery pack out level along its rails and set it on the table, hold the 3.4 kg trimmer by its front hoop, pull the 8 mm sprung rotation lever behind the rear grip, start the handle turning, let the lever go and push the handle on until it locks at 90 degrees.

## Why it matters

Landscaping and groundskeeping workers hold 952,640 US jobs (0.95 million) (SOC 37-3011, BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2024). O*NET lists as a Core task of the occupation (37-3011.00, importance 84): "Use hand tools, such as shovels, rakes, pruning saws, saws, hedge or brush trimmers, or axes." Turning the rear handle to its side stop is how a rotating-handle trimmer is readied to cut the side of a hedge; the HT60L00 manual (p.12) gives the steps: "Pull the rotation lever and begin turning the rear handle. Release the rotation lever and turn the handle 90 degrees to either side until it clicks into place and the lever locks in." It also asks for the battery to be removed "before inspecting, adjusting, or performing maintenance on any part of the hedge trimmer" (p.10).

## Fidelity

Rigid bodies on PhysX at real size and mass. The rear handle turns on one joint about the motor-housing axis with 0.5 N m of friction; a rule locks it when it is within 2 degrees of -90, 0 or 90 degrees (a scene tolerance; a real detent's seat is read on the bench by rocking the handle at its click with the lever at rest, protractor under the grip, and the unit is accepted when the rest position is within 2 degrees of the mark) and the lever is back at rest (under 2 mm), and frees it while the lever is pulled 6 mm or more. The lever is a sprung slider on the handle, 1250 N/m over 8 mm (10 N at its stop). The bay latch is a stand-in: a 150 N hold-down on the pack (a real latch is a small spring catch) that is released only when the release button (6 N at its stop) is pressed in 5 mm of its 6 mm travel; then the pack is pushed by 4 N along its rails for a 15 mm stroke (window 12 to 20 mm, below), as the manual's "This will cause the battery to eject from the tool." The pack is 170 mm long, so it stays mostly in the port and the hand must still draw it the rest of the way out level along the rails. The housing is modelled as a box and the pack as a box without its handle hump.

Lever force, button force, turn friction, eject force, eject stroke, rail friction and rail play have no published values. Bounds: MIL-STD-1472H gives 2.8 to 11 N for a finger-operated push button, and the lever, also worked by one finger, is taken in the same band; the turn friction must hold the 0.35 kg handle (centre about 0.1 m off the axis: 0.35 x 9.81 x 0.1 = 0.34 N m) from flopping while it is free, and a 6 N push on the grip at 0.1 m, inside the same one-finger band, must still turn it (0.6 N m). The scorer and demo pass at both ends (`TRIM_RANGE=lo`: 5 N lever, 3 N button, 0.3 N m; `TRIM_RANGE=hi`: 11 N lever, 11 N button, 0.6 N m). A real trial measures them with a push-pull force gauge on the lever paddle at its 8 mm stop (accepted 5 to 11 N) and on the release button at its stop (3 to 11 N), and with a spring scale pulled square on the rear grip, 0.1 m from the axis, while the lever is held (3 to 6 N, so 0.3 to 0.6 N m). The eject force is measured with a force gauge pushed against the pack's outer end, back toward its seat, while the button is held in (the eject force is an estimate, no published value: the force to slide the 1.24 kg pack against a rail friction of 0.25 to 0.4 is about 3.0 to 4.9 N; the scene uses 4 N, and the accepted window is about 4 to 14 N, run at `TRIM_EJECT=4` and `TRIM_EJECT=14`; a real unit is accepted on its own bench-check reading inside that window). The eject stroke is the distance the pack travels from latch release to rest. No maker value is published; the maker publishes no eject-travel figure, so the window is 12 to 20 mm: 12 mm is the shortest stroke at which the pack can be gripped and drawn out level (a pack left deeper in the port tips in the pinch), and 20 mm is the longest before the spring does much of the draw itself; the spring only has to free the pack from its catch and leave its end proud of the port to be gripped, while the pack's 170 mm length (118 mm tall, 83 mm wide, retail listing) and the 8 mm rail height leave it mostly seated. It is read with a steel rule or calipers against a tape mark on the housing at the pack's end, before and after pressing the button with the trimmer on the bench and the hand off the pack (accepted 12 to 20 mm). The real trial records the pack's pop-out distance this way before the first trial, and reports a unit that reads outside 12 to 20 mm as out of range. The scene uses 15 mm and the scorer and demo reach 1.0 at both ends (`TRIM_STROKE=lo`, 12 mm, and `TRIM_STROKE=hi`, 20 mm); a stroke much longer than 20 mm would do more of the draw by itself, and a shorter one than 12 mm leaves the pack too deep to grip level (10 mm failed in the scene), and the scorer's `ejectonly` route (press the button, then nothing) scores 0.0 at the default stroke and at the 20 mm end with the 14 N eject (`TRIM_STROKE=hi TRIM_EJECT=14`, 227 steps). The pack-on-rail friction coefficient is the pair value of the pack's plastic on the rail's plastic: an engineering estimate for plastic on plastic (ABS, polypropylene, nylon) of about 0.2 to 0.5, with no named source checked, and a pair over about 0.33 would hold the pack against the 4 N scene spring, so the scene runs 0.25 to 0.4 with `TRIM_EJECT=6` at 0.4. The scene's pair value is 0.275 (3.3 N holds the 1.24 kg pack at rest, so a 4 N eject moves it), run from 0.25 (`TRIM_RAILMU=lo`) to 0.4 (`TRIM_RAILMU=hi`, run at `TRIM_EJECT=6`, because 0.4 holds 4.9 N). A real unit measures it by tilting the trimmer about its blade axis (a board under one side) with the latch open and the pack on its rails, raising the board, starting only after the eject has stopped and the pack is at rest at the end of its stroke, until the pack just starts to slide, and reading the angle with a protractor or phone inclinometer: mu = tan(angle), accepted 0.25 to 0.4 (14 to 22 degrees). A unit outside that window is outside the range the scorer was run over: below 14 degrees the pack slides further when released, above 22 degrees the pack stays put under a 4 N spring, so `TRIM_EJECT` is raised to the measured force and the scorer rerun. In the scene the pack has its own material, about 0.5 (plastic on rubber finger pads and on the table), and only the rail pair's contact surfaces (the port's seat top and the two rails) carry a softer material, set so that the pack-on-rail pair comes to the stated 0.275; the demo grip is 40 N a finger, the YAM's rating. The rail play, 1 mm a side in the scene, is measured with a feeler gauge between the pack's side and each rail (accepted 0.2 to 1.5 mm a side; a tighter fit only makes the draw harder, and 1.5 mm is the largest fit clearance allowed).

## Real trial

**Bench setup.** Items: one Greenworks Pro HT60L00 trimmer (tool only) and one LB60A00 2.0 Ah pack (see Real items). Use a flat, rigid, level work table at least 1.2 m x 0.8 m. Lay the trimmer on it base down, the housing flat on the table, the blade cover on and pointing to the robot's right, as in Figure 4 of the manual. The rotating rear handle sits at its 0 degree (centre) stop. The blade axis is a straight line on the table; mark it with tape. Use the station frame: x forward from the arm bases, y to the robot's left, origin at the table's reference corner on the arm-base line. Put the rear-handle axis (the motor housing's rear face) at x 220 mm, y 260 mm, with the blade running toward -y (the robot's right); the scene uses these numbers. Check the blade line with a tape measure to within 5 mm and a protractor or a printed 0, 15 and 90 degree sheet under the handle. The pack is seated in the battery port, which is across the housing 140 mm from the rear face. Nothing else stands on the table. Do not clamp, tape or weigh the trimmer down: it rests on its own mass (3.4 kg without the pack, 4.64 kg with it). Mark the trimmer's housing outline on the table with tape so slide can be read from it.

**Reset between trials.** In this order: (1) take the pack from the table; (2) rotate the rear handle back to 0 degrees: pull the lever, turn, release, and confirm it clicks and the lever sits back at rest; (3) align the housing to its tape outline; (4) press the pack fully back into the port along its rails until the latch clicks; (5) check that the pack and button are as at the start (see the checks below).

**One trial.**
1. Before the first trial, measure the eject force: with the release button held in, push a force gauge on the pack's outer end back toward its seat and read the force (accepted 4 to 14 N); if the reading is outside that window, set `TRIM_EJECT` to the reading and rerun. Then start a stopwatch and a camera above the table.
2. Press the release button in at least 5 mm. The pack is pushed out along its rails by the eject spring.
3. Draw the pack out level along its rails (no lifting more than 10 mm while it is over the rails; read from a side camera (a phone, 30 frames per second or faster) at table height with a mm ruler in view beside the rails) and set it upright on the table, base down, clear of the trimmer.
4. Hold the trimmer by its front hoop with one hand so it does not slide or tilt.
5. With the other hand pull the rotation lever back at least 6 mm, start the rear handle turning, let the lever go and push the handle on to a click at 90 degrees to the left or right.
6. Take both hands off the trimmer and the pack, at least 20 mm clear, and hold them there 3 s. Stop the clock and take a photo.

**Scoring by hand (cumulative).**
- 0.25: pack off its rails and standing upright on its base. Check with a level or phone inclinometer on its top face (tilt under 20 degrees) and a ruler for its centre under 90 mm above the table. It must have been set down: its lowest corner under 30 mm above the table when the fingers let go, read from a side camera (a phone) at table height, mm ruler in view, filmed at 30 frames per second or faster (a phone's normal video; one frame is 33 ms, so a 30 mm drop is read to within a frame), played frame by frame (a fall faster than 0.77 m/s, free fall from 30 mm, is a drop).
- 0.5: rear handle at least 15 degrees off centre, read with the protractor sheet or a phone inclinometer on the grip.
- 0.75: handle clicked at 90 degrees (within 2 degrees, protractor) to either side, and the lever back at rest (under 2 mm, steel rule or calipers at the paddle).
- 1.0: also both hands, fingers to wrist, at least 20 mm from the trimmer and pack for 3 s (ruler, video).
- Score 0: the pack dropped; the handle turned more than 3 degrees with the pack still in the port; the pack lifted more than 10 mm while over the rails (side camera at table height, 30 frames per second or faster, mm ruler in view); the trimmer slid more than 100 mm (tape outline and tape measure), lifted more than 15 mm (ruler under a corner) or tilted more than 6 degrees (inclinometer on the housing).

**Battery release force.** The release button needs about 6 N at its 6 mm travel stop in the scene (the latch frees at 5 mm in), and the pack is pushed out by 4 N of spring for 15 mm, with a 0.275 friction coefficient on its rails. The eject force is an estimate, no published value: the force to slide the 1.24 kg pack against a rail friction of 0.25 to 0.4 is about 3.0 to 4.9 N; the button force is an estimate with no published value. Bench check, with a push-pull force gauge (0 to 20 N, 0.1 N resolution): push the gauge square onto the release button and read the force at its stop (accepted 3 to 11 N, the one-finger band of MIL-STD-1472H). Measure the eject spring from the outside: with the button held in, push the pack back toward its seat with the gauge on the pack's outer end and read the force that holds it against the spring (accepted about 4 to 14 N). If a real unit reads outside that window, set `TRIM_EJECT` to the measured value and rerun the scorer. The scorer and demo pass at both ends (`TRIM_RANGE=lo` and `hi`, `TRIM_EJECT=4` and `14`). The pack's hold-down in the scene, 150 N, stands for a small spring catch; the check is that the pack cannot be drawn out by hand without pressing the button.

**Rail and latch checks.** (a) With the pack seated, pull it along the rails without pressing the button: hook a 0 to 200 N luggage scale on the pack and pull along the rails at a steady 10 mm/s, ramping the force by hand over about 15 s: the pack must not move (under 0.5 mm on a dial gauge) up to 150 N (accepted: holds at 100 N or more, the firm hand pull; 150 N is the scene's stand-in), or the latch is too weak for the scene's no-button route. (b) Slide a feeler gauge between each pack side and its rail: 0.2 to 1.5 mm a side (the scene uses 1 mm). (c) Measure the rail height with calipers: 8 mm in the scene. (d) Measure the lever's travel from rest to its stop with calipers (8 mm in the scene, accepted 6 to 10 mm) and the paddle's width (15 mm). Pull the rotation lever with the gauge on its paddle at its 8 mm stop (accepted 5 to 11 N), and pull the rear grip square with a spring scale 0.1 m from the axis while the lever is held (3 to 6 N, so 0.3 to 0.6 N m). With the lever at rest, a push of 6 N on the grip must not turn the handle.
